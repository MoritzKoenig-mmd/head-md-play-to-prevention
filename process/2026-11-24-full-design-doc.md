# Without a trace: Game Design Document (Full)
 
Sarah Courtade, Adam Hudec, Moritz König
 
## Overview
 
A story about the signs of cyberbullying, seen through the eyes of a younger sibling who secretly borrows the older one's confiscated Nintendo 3DS.
 
## Pitch
 
Every night, 8-year-old Coco sneaks off with the 3DS their parents took away from their older sibling Vivi. The console is Vivi's treasure and a reflection of who they are: the stickers on the case, the games, the online chats, the photos. Going through it night by night, Coco slowly realises Vivi is being bullied and struggling with their identity. Coco has to choose: tell someone or stay silent.
 
|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| Platform | Web, mobile first |
| Genre | Educational interactive story about cyberbullying |
| Length | 30 to 40 minutes: 6 or 8 nights, about 7 minutes per night |
 
## Prevention
 
### The dilemma
 
Coco finds out Vivi is being bullied, but only because they secretly went through Vivi's console. Adults only see the symptoms: bad grades, secrecy, withdrawal.
 
Many young people who know about bullying stay quiet. They fear betraying someone, being called a snitch, or getting into trouble themselves. Coco faces the same choice: telling means admitting they invaded Vivi's privacy and risking Vivi's trust, staying silent lets things get worse for Vivi.
 
### Learning objectives
 
After playing, players can:
 
- Spot the warning signs: withdrawal, slipping grades, secrecy, always being on a device, anxiety about others seeing it
- Understand why victims stay silent
- Understand that telling a trusted adult is not betrayal
- Know how to help: tell a trusted adult, save evidence, block and report, support the person being bullied
- Know where to get help
## Gameplay pillars
 
1. **The choice is the core.** Tell or stay silent. The number of clues found shapes the ending.
2. **Loyalty has a price.** Telling means admitting Coco went through Vivi's console, risking Vivi's trust and shattering the perfect-family image the parents hold on to. Staying silent lets Vivi's situation get worse.
3. **The 3DS is the whole world.** Each night the game cuts straight to the console screen. The player chooses what to open and links clues across apps in their head. The console is taken away at night, so Vivi adds new content by day.
4. **Discovery through intrusion.** Coco knows more than the adults only because they broke Vivi's privacy.
5. **Silence protects no one.** Only action can start the healing.
## Story, setting and characters
 
Coco secretly takes Vivi's 3DS every night and slowly uncovers clues that Vivi is being bullied and struggling with their identity. The parents work hard to keep up the image of a perfect family. Vivi's problems don't fit into it, which is why Vivi stays silent.
 
At one point Vivi searches for the 3DS and gets caught by the parents. Coco witnesses the punishment.
 
Coco must decide whether to tell. Telling means admitting the intrusion and risking Vivi's trust. Staying silent lets things get worse, until Vivi runs away from home.
 
| Character | Age | Role and agency |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coco | 8 | Player character. Holds the core choice: tell or stay silent. |
| Vivi | 12 | Coco's older sibling and owner of the 3DS. Little agency: silence, escape, and anger acted out in a game by shooting the bullies' faces. |
| Parents |  | Confiscated the 3DS. Hold on to the perfect-family image, an unspoken rule that restrains both siblings. |
| Classmates |  | Laugh along. They understand what is happening and do nothing. |
 
## Core mechanics
 
### Time
 
The story runs over 6 or 8 nights. Each night has an internal timer that is not shown, or only implied, on screen. Clues have to be found within a few minutes, and the night ends when the timer runs out. Vivi adds new content to the console by day, so every night shows a changed console.
 
### Clues
 
- Each night, 2 of the 4 clue spots hold a clue
- 14 clues in total, 8 needed to unlock the good ending
- Missed clues do not reappear, but they transform into new clues
- No visible score or checklist of clues
- A found clue triggers a comment from Coco and an audiovisual cue


| Clue spot | What changes |
| --- | --- |
| Stickers on the 3DS case | Ripped or replaced |
| Mii avatar menu | Avatar appearance changes |
| Game chat | Keyboard word suggestions |
| Doodle gallery | New drawings |
 
Further candidate clue carriers: home screen visuals and design, games, notes, animations, texts.
 
### Choice and endings
 
The gameplay and story lead up to the end of the last night. There, Coco makes the choice, and the game runs a simple check on how many clues were found:
 
- **8 or more clues:** Coco can tell or stay silent
- **Fewer than 8 clues:** telling is shown as an option but greyed out, so Coco can only stay silent
| Choice | Ending |
| --- | --- |
| Tell a teacher at school that Vivi is being bullied | Good ending: the teacher contacts the people responsible for dealing with the bullying. The parents find out, and Vivi learns that Coco went through the console. There is tension between the siblings, but in the end it strengthens their bond. Vivi remakes their Mii avatar. |
| Stay silent | Bad ending: Vivi runs away from home and leaves a note. |
 
## Visuals and unique features
 
The player sees only the 3DS. The story is told through the console: the object itself and what is on its screen.
 
- **Virtual interactive console.** All on-screen content is Vivi's: games, chats with online friends, drawings, notes, photos.
- **Digital stickers as a mechanic.** Stickers can be put onto the digital console. The stickers on the case reflect who Vivi is, bringing a physical object into the virtual.
## Target audience and classroom use
 
The game serves young people who know about bullying. When facing a choice in real life, they can learn how to approach the situation.
 
|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| Age | 12 to 16 |
| Gender | Not defined |
| Who | School students; teachers lead the class exercise |
| Values | Loyalty to friends and siblings. Many don't want to betray someone, look like a snitch, or get into trouble themselves. |
| Where | At school and at home |
 
**In class:** students play individually, then do a class exercise and discussion led by the teacher, supported by an educational worksheet.
