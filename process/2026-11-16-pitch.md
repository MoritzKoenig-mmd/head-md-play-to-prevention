# Without a trace

## Pitch

Every night, 8-year-old Coco sneaks off with the Nintendo 3DS their parents took away from their older sibling Vivi. The console is Vivi’s treasure and a reflection of who they are – the stickers on the case, the games, the online chats, the photos. Day by day, Coco uncovers clues that Vivi is being bullied and struggling with their identity. Coco notices and slowly realizes Vivi is being bullied.

## What is the experience about

The game is about loyalty. Coco secretly takes Vivi’s 3DS every night and slowly realises Vivi is being bullied. Their parents work hard to keep up the image of a perfect family, and Vivi’s problems don’t fit into it. That is also why Vivi stays silent.

If Coco tells someone, they have to admit they went through Vivi’s console, they could lose Vivi’s trust, and they would shatter the image the parents are holding on to. If they stay silent, Vivi’s situation gets worse, and Vivi might eventually take their own life.

Many young people who know about bullying keep quiet. They don't want to betray a friend or sibling, look like a snitch, or get into trouble themselves. Coco faces the same choice. The game asks whether telling is a betrayal, or a way of protecting someone.

Adults only see the symptoms: bad grades, secrecy, withdrawal. Coco knows more only because he broke Vivi's privacy. Coco faces a choice whether to tell somebody about what's happening to Vivi or stay silent. Silence protects no one; only action can start the healing.


## What is understood / What knowledge is learned

- Spot the warning signs: withdrawal, slipping grades, secrecy, always being on a device, anxiety about others seeing it
- Understand why victims stay silent
- Understand that telling a trusted adult is not betrayal
- Know how to help: tell a trusted adult, save evidence, block and report, support the person being bullied
- Know where to get help [check the right helplines]


## Agency

Coco's choice is the core of the game: tell or stay silent.

Vivi has little agency: silence, escape, and anger acted out in a game by shooting the bullies' faces.

Both siblings feel stuck. The unspoken rule of keeping the “perfect family” image is restraining them.

The classmates who laugh along understand what is happening and do nothing.

Players who face the same choice in real life can make it differently.


## How is your experience played?
It can be played alone at home or at school. At school, students play individually, then do a class exercise and discussion led by a teacher, supported by a engaging educational worksheet.


## Questions for the cool kids

- If you found out a sibling or friend was being bullied, would you tell someone? Who?
- Do you sometimes feel that it's better to keep certain things to yourself?
- Do you already regret having spoken out for someone else or for yourself?
- Would you like to consult someone's diary to help them?
- Do you think all topics should be discussed within the family?
- What are some topics you would prefer to avoid discussing with your family?
